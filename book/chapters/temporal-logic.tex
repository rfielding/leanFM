\chapter{CTL over Generated Futures}
\label{ch:ctl}

\index{CTL}
\index{temporal logic}
\index{LTL}
\index{modal logic}
A well-formed global type already guarantees safety, fidelity and progress under
its channels' assumptions. Everything else a stakeholder wants to know is a
question about futures: can the transfer always still finish, must it, can this
message ever precede that one? The global type and its channel physics generate a
branching transition system, and CTL speaks directly about that branching: path
quantifiers distinguish \emph{some future} from \emph{every future}, temporal
operators the next state, eventuality, invariance and until. The author learned
the modal logic used here mostly from \citet{kroger2008temporal}.

\section{Basic modal logic}

Propositional logic evaluates a formula at one world or state. Basic modal logic
adds an accessibility relation $R$ between worlds and two operators:

\AccessibleEquation{World w satisfies box p if and only if p holds at every world v such that w is related to v. World w satisfies diamond p if and only if p holds at some world v such that w is related to v.}{\begin{align*}
  w \models \Box p &\quad\text{iff}\quad
    \text{$p$ holds at every $v$ such that $wRv$},\\
  w \models \Diamond p &\quad\text{iff}\quad
    \text{$p$ holds at some $v$ such that $wRv$}.
\end{align*}}

$\Box p$ and $\Diamond p$ read ``necessarily $p$'' and ``possibly $p$'', and are
dual: $\Diamond p \equiv \neg\Box\neg p$. The symbols do not fix what accessibility
means: a next computation state, a world compatible with an actor's knowledge, a
permitted world. The basic logic K allows any frame; reflexivity, transitivity,
symmetry and the Euclidean condition give stronger systems, whose principles must
not be imported because ``necessarily'' sounds strong. LeanFM's relation is a
directed computation successor, not reflexive, symmetric or epistemic.

\section{LTL: time along one path}

Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) describes one path with $X$ (next), $F$
(eventually), $G$ (always) and $U$ (until): $F p$ says $p$ holds at some suffix of
the path, $G p$ at every suffix. Its operators answer \emph{where along this
path}, not whether one branch or every branch is meant; the common convention that
every initial path must satisfy the formula is outside the syntax. ``Some legal
execution eventually recovers'' and ``every one does'' are different claims that
share the words ``eventually recovery''.

LeanFM does not implement LTL. It uses CTL because requirements combine two
independent axes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{always} or \emph{eventually} says where along forward time a
        predicate must hold; and
  \item \emph{necessarily} or \emph{possibly} says whether it must hold on every
        permitted forward continuation or may hold on at least one.
\end{itemize}

The construction is \emph{temporally alethic}: possible means possible now or at
some point along a legal future. English uses ``always'' for both universal
modality and temporal persistence; CTL keeps the two coordinates apart.

\section{CTL: path modality combined with time}

CTL pairs a path quantifier with each temporal operator: $E$, some permitted
continuation (temporal possibility), or $A$, every one (temporal necessity). They
quantify over legal computations from the current observation, not timeless
worlds. $F$ versus $G$ is eventuality versus invariance along a continuation; $E$
versus $A$ is some versus all continuations. The four core combinations and their
dualities:

\AccessibleEquation{E F p means possibly eventually p. A F p is equivalent to not E G not p. E G p means p always on some future. A G p is equivalent to not E F not p.}{\begin{align*}
 EF\,p &: \text{possibly eventually $p$}, &
 AF\,p &\equiv \neg EG\,\neg p : \text{on every future, eventually $p$},\\
 EG\,p &: \text{$p$ always on some future}, &
 AG\,p &\equiv \neg EF\,\neg p : \text{$p$ holds on every future at every position}.
\end{align*}}

$A$ ranges over futures and $G$ over positions along one. The dualities drive
counterexample search: $AG\,p$ is tested by searching for $EF\,\neg p$, $AF\,p$ by
searching for $EG\,\neg p$. A witness refutes the universal property; its absence
establishes it only within the finite checked model.

\subsection{Least upper bounds and greatest lower bounds}

Eventuality and strong until choose the least solution of their recursive
equations. Over the lattice of state sets, begin with the empty set and
repeatedly add states justified by the previous approximation:

\AccessibleEquation{The states satisfying E F p are the least fixed point of Z equal P union existential predecessor of Z. This fixed point is the union or least upper bound of approximations Z zero Z one and onward.}{\[
  \operatorname{Sat}(EF\,p)
  = \mu Z.\bigl(P\cup\operatorname{Pre}_{\exists}(Z)\bigr)
  = \bigcup_{n\ge 0} Z_n,
  \qquad Z_0=\varnothing.
\]}

The same construction gives existential until:

\AccessibleEquation{The states satisfying p E U q are the least fixed point of Z equal Q union P intersect existential predecessor of Z.}{\[
  \operatorname{Sat}(p\ EU\ q)
  = \mu Z.\bigl(Q\cup(P\cap\operatorname{Pre}_{\exists}(Z))\bigr).
\]}

$AF$ and strong $AU$ are least fixed points too, with the universal predecessor, so
a cycle that never reaches the target adds nothing. Invariance and weak until make
the dual choice: start from all candidate states and remove unsupported ones,
giving the greatest fixed point:

\AccessibleEquation{The states satisfying E G p are the greatest fixed point of Z equal P intersect existential predecessor of Z. This fixed point is the intersection or greatest lower bound of descending approximations.}{\[
  \operatorname{Sat}(EG\,p)
  = \nu Z.\bigl(P\cap\operatorname{Pre}_{\exists}(Z)\bigr)
  = \bigcap_{n\ge 0} Y_n.
\]}

LeanFM has two evaluators, and tests check that they agree. The recursive one in
\texttt{LeanFM.CTL}, used by task metrics, searches with cycle awareness: an
unresolved revisit is false for \texttt{EF}, \texttt{EU} and strong \texttt{AU}
(least fixed point) and a predicate-preserving revisit true for \texttt{EG} and
weak until (greatest), with \texttt{AG} as $\neg EF\,\neg p$ and \texttt{AF} as
$\neg EG\,\neg p$. The one in \texttt{LeanFM.Logic}, behind the MCP
\texttt{check\_ctl} tool, labels every reachable node with the set satisfying each
subformula by fixed-point iteration, and returns a witness or counterexample
path.

\subsection{Asymptotic cost}

Let $V$ be the reachable observations, $E$ the edges, $L$ the formula's nodes and
$P$ the simple paths a search inspects. The recursive evaluator keeps a path-local
list of visited states and memoizes nothing, so one temporal search costs
$O(E_P+VP)$ time ($E_P$ edge visits over those paths) and $O(V+L)$ memory: it may
revisit a state once per path reaching it, and $P$ can exceed $2^V$ in a dense
graph. The fixed-point evaluator avoids this: each fixed point takes at most $V+1$
iterations of $O(V+E)$, so a formula costs $O(L\,V\,(V+E))$, within its bound of
4096 nodes. Worklist propagation would reach the textbook $O(L(V+E))$; that is a
target.

None of this compares LeanFM with TLC. LeanFM often builds a smaller graph by
omitting private variables, but a well-abstracted TLA+ model may omit them too, and
a speed claim would need the same protocol, properties, bounds, hardware and
settings. The book claims only a different modeling boundary.

An observation is immutable once recorded: values change only on a forward
transition to a successor, though they need not be monotone.

$AF\,p$ requires $p$ on every legal future from the current prefix; $EF\,p$ needs
one future as a witness. Branching is part of the requirement. Every temporal
operator in CTL is governed by $A$ or $E$; CTL*, which nests state and path
formulas freely, is not implemented, so not every English alternation of ``on all
paths'' and ``eventually'' is a formula here.

\section{Projecting to LTL-style F and G}

Requirements often want the simpler forms $F p$ and $G p$, which LeanFM shows only
with an explicit path policy. On one selected execution, $AF p$ and $EF p$ both
project to $F p$ and $AG p$ and $EG p$ to $G p$; under the convention that every
initial path must satisfy the formula, $F p$ is $AF p$ and $G p$ is $AG p$.
Displaying $AF$ and $AG$ as $F$ and $G$ is an interface reduction, not an
equivalence: the artifact keeps the exact CTL, so the checker can still use
witnesses, negation and the right counterexample search.

\section{Persistence and recurrence}

Nested CTL operators express two common requirements:
\AccessibleEquation{A F A G p: on every future it eventually becomes true that p remains true forever. A G A F p: on every future p occurs again eventually.}{\[
 AF\,AG\,p, \qquad AG\,AF\,p.
\]}
The first is eventual permanent safety: every path reaches a state from which
every path keeps $p$ (finish key rotation and never accept the old key again). The
second is universal recurrence, ``always eventually $p$'' (service keeps returning
to ready). For witness searches their duals are:
\AccessibleEquation{Not A F A G p is equivalent to E G E F not p. Not A G A F p is equivalent to E F E G not p.}{\[
 \neg(AF\,AG\,p)\equiv EG\,EF\,\neg p,\qquad
 \neg(AG\,AF\,p)\equiv EF\,EG\,\neg p.
\]}
The existential forms are $EF\,EG\,p$ and $EG\,EF\,p$; in the second, the eventual
witnesses may leave the outer path, so it does not require infinitely many $p$ on
that path. These cover useful persistence and recurrence, not all LTL: $G F p$
corresponds to $AG\,AF\,p$ on a total transition model, but $AF\,AG\,p$ is
stronger than pathwise $F G p$, since it needs a state where \emph{all}
continuations stay safe. Terminal states follow the checker's continuation
convention.

\section{Knowledge, obligation, and permission}

A multimodal model can have several accessibility relations: temporal reachability,
$K_a/\widehat K_a$ for what actor $a$ knows or considers possible, another pair for
permission and obligation. They are not interchangeable. LeanFM's $A$ and $E$
range only over legal forward computations of the current model. They do not mean:

\begin{itemize}
  \item probability one versus positive probability;
  \item known versus merely believed by an actor;
  \item obligatory versus permitted by a policy; or
  \item physically necessary versus logically possible.
\end{itemize}

Knowledge and policy are modelling techniques a project defines with the relations
below, not built-in operators (Chapter~\ref{ch:csp-exercises} decides the
state-local \texttt{knows} predicate). The Kerberos video~\citep{leanfmKerberosVideo}
illustrates them; it does not verify the protocol.

For knowledge, let $W$ be the declared set of legal histories and
$\operatorname{obs}_a(h)$ actor $a$'s local observation of history $h$:
\AccessibleEquation{Actor a knows phi at history h if and only if for every history h prime in W: if actor a has the same observation at h and h prime then phi holds at h prime.}{\[
 K_a\varphi(h) \iff
 \forall h'\in W.\;
 (\operatorname{obs}_a(h)=\operatorname{obs}_a(h')\Rightarrow\varphi(h')).
\]}
Observations declare visible messages, private keys, clock information and
retained memory. With the current history in $W$, knowledge is factive; belief needs
a different relation and must not inherit that guarantee.
\AccessibleEquation{It is epistemically possible for actor a that phi if and only if actor a does not know not phi.}{\[
 \widehat K_a\varphi \equiv \neg K_a\neg\varphi.
\]}
This is epistemic possibility, not temporal reachability.

For policy, let $\operatorname{Ideal}_a(h)$ be the declared policy-compliant
futures from $h$, and let $\psi$ be a condition on a whole future path:
\AccessibleEquation{Actor a is obligated to ensure psi at history h if and only if psi holds on every path pi that is ideal for actor a at h. Actor a is permitted to ensure psi at history h if and only if psi holds on some path pi that is ideal for actor a at h.}{\[
 O_a\psi(h)\iff\forall\pi\in\operatorname{Ideal}_a(h).\;\psi(\pi),
 \qquad
 P_a\psi(h)\iff\exists\pi\in\operatorname{Ideal}_a(h).\;\psi(\pi).
\]}
The set of ideal futures must be nonempty, so contradictory policies are reported
rather than certified vacuously. Legal executions may violate policy, and detecting
that is part of the analysis; these are obligations under a named policy, not moral
judgments.
\AccessibleEquation{Actor a is permitted to ensure psi if and only if actor a is not obligated to ensure not psi.}{\[
 P_a\psi\equiv\neg O_a\neg\psi.
\]}
when both use the same policy relation. For example:
\AccessibleEquation{Actor a is obligated eventually to audit record r at history h if and only if for every path pi ideal for actor a at h there exists a nonnegative index i such that audit of r holds at state pi sub i.}{\[
 O_a F\,\operatorname{audit}(r)(h)\iff
 \forall\pi\in\operatorname{Ideal}_a(h).\;\exists i\geq0.\;
 \operatorname{audit}(r,\pi_i).
\]}
Here $F$ is a path operator, kept distinct from state formulas. Temporal, epistemic
and policy quantification then combine without confusing their domains: a legal
future may be possible, unknown to Alice, and forbidden by policy at once.
Probability, belief, strategic ability and program-indexed modalities remain
separate work, and symbolic uncompromised-key assumptions do not establish
computational cryptographic security.

\section{Possibility means preparedness}

A requirement that declares $EF\,p$ does more than admit that a checker can find a
path to $p$: it says $p$ is a supported future the system must prepare for. A
\texttt{possibly} obligation therefore links three artifacts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a legal grammar branch and witness trace reaching the condition;
  \item a handling plan in \texttt{Requirements.lean}; and
  \item generated implementation code whose justification cites that
        possibility requirement.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent This is the target; today validation checks only that a handling plan
is cited, not that generated code exists or covers the witness.

For example, ``a bakery delivery may fail'' requires a decodable
\texttt{DeliveryFailed} message, a legal failure branch, and code that performs
the specified refund or notification behavior. Removing every failure branch
does not implement reliability; it makes the real failure trace out-of-spec.

Possibility is not probability: a possible condition may be rare or of unknown
likelihood, and the handling obligation comes from its being supported, not from a
percentage crossing a threshold. $EG\,p$ carries a different obligation: some
future keeps $p$, so the system monitors the conditions that keep that future
viable, exposes their loss, and controls choices that would destroy it. That every
execution keeps $p$ is the stronger $AG\,p$.

\section{Strong implication and the Chewbacca argument}

The ``Chewbacca defense'', from the \emph{South Park} episode ``Chef Aid'', is a
courtroom parody that uses an irrelevant claim to distract from the conclusion: a
memorable non sequitur. Material implication $p\mathbin{\to}q$ is true whenever $p$
is false, which permits a vacuous global claim when no reachable state satisfies
$p$. LeanFM names the non-vacuous combination

\AccessibleEquation{p strongly implies q is defined as E F p and A G of p implies q. In words: p is reachable and whenever p holds q also holds.}{\[
  p\mathbin{\Rightarrow_s}q
  \quad\stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\quad
  EF\,p \;\land\; AG(p\mathbin{\to}q).
\]}

It requires a legal future witnessing $p$ and that every reachable $p$ also
satisfies $q$, so together they imply $EF\,q$. The absurd test:

\AccessibleEquation{p is defined as Chewbacca lives on Endor. q is defined as you must acquit.}{\begin{align*}
  p &= \text{``Chewbacca lives on Endor''},\\
  q &= \text{``you must acquit''}.
\end{align*}}

Mentioning $p$ does not obtain $q$: the model must reach a $p$ witness and
establish $AG(p\to q)$ on every continuation, and if the grammar relates residence
to nothing, that obligation fails. Logic cannot judge the relevance of English
propositions, but neither a missing antecedent nor one rhetorical occurrence of it
can make the implication pass vacuously.

In Lean the definition is direct:

\begin{lstlisting}
def stronglyImplies (p q : CTL S) : CTL S :=
  CTL.and (CTL.ef p) (CTL.ag (CTL.implies p q))
\end{lstlisting}

The path quantifier is part of the until operator: \texttt{AU} and \texttt{EU} are
binary operators, not an outer \texttt{A} or \texttt{E} around a bracketed until, so
a subexpression never changes meaning because of what surrounds it:

\begin{lstlisting}
def boundedUntilTerminal : CTL Observation :=
  atom withinCapacity AU atom isTerminal

def someAuthorizedContinuation : CTL Observation :=
  atom hasValidAuth EU atom isSucceeded
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Weak until: AW and EW}

Strong until requires the right operand to occur; weak until allows it never to,
provided the left operand keeps holding:

\AccessibleEquation{p A W q means: on every future p holds until q or p remains forever. p E W q means: on some future p holds until q or p remains forever.}{\begin{align*}
  p\ AW\ q &: \text{on every future, $p$ holds until $q$, or $p$ remains forever},\\
  p\ EW\ q &: \text{on some future, $p$ holds until $q$, or $p$ remains forever}.
\end{align*}}

The state satisfying $q$ need not satisfy $p$. Weak until fits ``remain available
until shutdown, which is permitted but not required''. $p\ EW\ q$ equals
$(p\ EU\ q)\lor EG\,p$, but $(p\ AU\ q)\lor AG\,p$ is \emph{not} $AW$ on a
branching system: some futures may reach $q$ while others keep $p$ forever, so
neither disjunct holds although every future satisfies weak until. LeanFM
implements \texttt{AW} directly.

\begin{lstlisting}
def serviceMayRunUntilShutdown : CTL Observation :=
  atom available AW atom shutdown

def someWorkerCanRemainReady : CTL Observation :=
  atom ready EW atom retired
\end{lstlisting}

In \texttt{r \&\& (p AU q)} the right operand is the whole \texttt{AU} expression.

\section{Past claims}

\index{past-time logic}
CTL looks forward, but security claims often cite the past: ``this capability was
previously issued'', ``this response matches an earlier request''. Since states
are trace prefixes, past facts are predicates over the accumulated trace. Bounded
past conditions can be compiled into forward monitor state, evaluated before the
monitor takes the current event; on concurrent histories, ``before'' must be
declared as causal ancestry or a chosen linearization, never screen order. Whether
explicit past operators read and check better than trace predicates or monitors is
open, judged by how specifications survive changes to the event schema.

\section{Fairness and time bounds}

$AF$ alone does not distinguish a participant perpetually denied a turn from one
that violates the protocol, so fairness assumptions are named, scoped and visible
in theorem statements. ``Eventually'' is not ``within 200 milliseconds'': time
bounds belong in an explicit timed layer, not in timestamps and prose.
